The **Berlin Mathematical School (BMS)** is a joint graduate school of Berlin's three major universities: **Freie Universität Berlin, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and Technische Universität Berlin**. It is one of the best places in Europe to train as a research mathematician.

The BMS programme has two phases:
- **Phase I:** a master's-level programme with advanced courses in mathematics (about 2 years)
- **Phase II:** doctoral research leading to a PhD

**Phase I scholarships** support talented students who are starting this path with a bachelor's degree.

## What the scholarship covers

- **EUR 1,000 per month** for living costs in Berlin
- Paid for **18 months (3 semesters)**, with the option of a **6-month extension** for a 4th semester, applied for during your 3rd semester
- **No tuition fees** at Berlin's public universities (students pay only a small semester fee, which includes a public transport ticket)

About **10 Phase I scholarships** are awarded in each first round.

## Who can apply

- Students of **any nationality**
- You will finish a **bachelor's degree in mathematics** (or a closely related field with a strong maths focus) by **summer 2027**
- You have an excellent academic record in mathematics
- You are strongly motivated to do research and plan to continue to a PhD

Students who speak good German are usually expected to fund themselves through other means, such as student jobs or other scholarships. The BMS courses are taught in English.

## Documents you need

- Academic transcripts
- CV
- Motivation letter
- Reference letters

## How to apply

1. Go to the **BMS online application portal**.
2. Choose **Phase I** and say that you are applying **with funding**.
3. Upload your transcripts, CV and motivation letter.
4. Ask your referees to submit their reference letters.
5. Submit before **1 December 2026**.

You can only get a Phase I scholarship by applying in the **first round**. If you are admitted without a scholarship, you cannot apply for one later.

## Deadlines

- **1 December 2026:** Phase I with scholarship (first round, 1 October – 1 December)
- **1 April 2027:** Phase I without funding (second round)
- Studies start in **October 2027**

## Tips

- In your motivation letter, name the areas of mathematics that interest you and any research or projects you have done.
- Ask referees who know your mathematical ability well, and give them time to write strong letters.
- Look at the research groups at FU, HU and TU Berlin, and mention the ones that match your interests.
