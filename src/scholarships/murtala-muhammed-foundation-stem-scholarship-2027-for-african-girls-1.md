The **Murtala Muhammed Foundation (MMF)**, a Nigerian non-profit founded in memory of the late General Murtala Muhammed, runs a **STEM scholarship for young African women** together with the **African Gifted Foundation**. Scholars study at the **African Science Academy (ASA)** in **Tema, Ghana**, a girls' school that offers **Cambridge A-Levels in Mathematics, Further Mathematics and Physics** on full scholarship.

The aim is to help gifted girls from across Africa, especially from low-income families, build careers in science, technology, engineering and maths.

## What the scholarship offers

- **Full scholarship** covering tuition, accommodation and living costs
- A **dedicated mentor**
- A **paid internship**
- A **leadership summer school**

Some websites describe this as a full 4-year university scholarship. The African Science Academy's own site describes a full scholarship for Cambridge A-Levels. Confirm exactly what is covered on the official websites before you apply.

## Who can apply

- **African** and **female**
- Aged **16 to 19**
- You have **completed secondary school** (Senior High School, WAEC, IGCSE, O-Level or equivalent)
- You have an **A or B in Mathematics and Physics** in WAEC or an equivalent exam
- A real **passion for STEM**
- **IELTS is not required**

## Documents you need

- Completed application form
- Academic transcripts or exam results
- A passport photograph
- Answers to the essay questions
- A recommendation letter from a Maths or Science teacher (optional, but helpful)

## How to apply

1. Visit the official **Murtala Muhammed Foundation** or **African Gifted Foundation** website and find the current scholarship call.
2. Read the eligibility rules and guidelines carefully.
3. Fill in the application form and answer the essay questions.
4. Upload your results, transcripts and passport photograph.
5. Submit before the deadline.

Use only the link on the official websites. Do not use forms shared by unknown pages.

## Deadline

**30 November 2026** (from the most recent listing). Earlier rounds closed in early January, so confirm the exact date on the official website.

## Tips

- In your essays, show your love for science and maths with real examples, such as a project, competition or problem you solved.
- Ask a Maths or Physics teacher who knows you well for a recommendation.
- Applying is free. Never pay anyone to "process" your scholarship.
