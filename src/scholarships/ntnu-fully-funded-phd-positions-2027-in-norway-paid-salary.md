The **Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU)** is Norway's largest university, with campuses in **Trondheim, Gjøvik and Ålesund**. It is known worldwide for engineering, technology, natural sciences and medicine.

In Norway, **PhD candidates are not students on a scholarship. They are employees.** That means a PhD position at NTNU is a **paid job** with a full salary and benefits, open to applicants of **any nationality**.

## What you get

- A salary of about **NOK 550,800 per year** before tax (about EUR 47,000), depending on the position and your experience
- **No tuition fees** for PhD candidates
- Membership of the **Norwegian Public Service Pension Fund** (2% of your salary goes to your pension)
- **Paid holidays**, parental leave and health care through the Norwegian system
- **Free basic Norwegian language classes** in many departments
- Some positions offer a **4th year** with teaching or other duties

**Duration:** usually 3 years, sometimes 4

## Who can apply

- Applicants of **any nationality**
- A **master's degree** (or equal) in a field relevant to the position, usually with at least 120 credits at master's level
- An average grade of **B or better** on NTNU's grading scale (or equivalent)
- **Very good English**, spoken and written. Some positions ask for a test score, for example TOEFL or IELTS.
- You must be eligible for admission to the relevant NTNU PhD programme

Some positions also accept students who will finish their master's degree soon. Read each advert carefully.

## Documents you usually need

- CV
- Master's and bachelor's diplomas and transcripts
- **Diploma Supplement** or a description of your grading system (for degrees from outside Norway)
- A short statement of motivation or research interests
- Names of **2 referees**
- English test results, if required
- Sometimes a research proposal and a signed supervisor confirmation

## How to apply

1. Go to **Jobbnorge.no** and search for **"NTNU PhD"** or **"PhD Candidate"**.
2. Pick a position that matches your master's degree and interests.
3. Read the advert carefully. Each one has its own requirements and deadline.
4. Prepare your documents in English.
5. Click **"Apply for this job"**, create an account and submit before the deadline.

Shortlisted applicants are usually interviewed online.

## Deadlines

There is **no single deadline**. NTNU posts new PhD positions throughout the year, and each one usually stays open for about 3–6 weeks. Check Jobbnorge every week.

## Other ways to study at NTNU with funding

- **Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's programmes** that include NTNU can offer full scholarships for master's students.
- **Quota-free and partner programmes** may be available through specific departments or international projects.

## Tips

- Apply only for positions that closely match your master's degree. Norwegian employers look for a clear fit.
- Make your CV short and clear, and list your research experience, publications and technical skills.
- Applying is free. Never pay anyone for an "NTNU scholarship".
