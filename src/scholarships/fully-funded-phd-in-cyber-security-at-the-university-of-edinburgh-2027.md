The \*\*University of Edinburgh\*\* in Scotland is home to one of the UK's leading computer science schools, the \*\*School of Informatics\*\*. Its \*\*PhD in Cyber Security, Privacy and Trust\*\* trains researchers to tackle problems like secure systems, privacy protection and trustworthy technology. According to the university, \*\*most PhD students in Informatics receive full scholarships\*\* covering tuition fees and living costs.

\> \*\*Good to know:\*\* there isn't one single "cyber security scholarship" at Edinburgh. PhD students are funded through \*\*School of Informatics scholarships\*\* and \*\*research scholarships for international students\*\*. Funding is competitive, and the \*\*2027 funding deadlines haven't been announced yet\*\*. Applications for the PhD itself are open all year.

\## About the PhD

\- \*\*Degree:\*\* PhD in Cyber Security, Privacy and Trust

\- \*\*Length:\*\* 3 years full-time (part-time options also exist)

\- \*\*Start dates:\*\* several during the year

\- \*\*School:\*\* School of Informatics, University of Edinburgh

\- \*\*No application fee\*\*

\## Funding available

The PhD programme page lists:

\- \*\*School of Informatics scholarships\*\* for research students

\- \*\*Research scholarships for international students\*\*

\- Other University scholarships, some of which need a \*\*separate application\*\* through the University's scholarships portal

Full scholarships usually cover \*\*tuition fees and living costs\*\*. For reference, the university estimates living costs in Edinburgh at about \*\*£18,504 a year\*\* (around £1,542 a month).

\## Who can apply

\- A \*\*UK 2:1 honours degree\*\* or international equivalent in \*\*computer science, mathematics, linguistics\*\* or a related subject

\- A \*\*master's in information security, cyber security\*\* or a closely related field is \*\*recommended\*\*

\- \*\*English:\*\* IELTS Academic \*\*6.5 overall\*\*, with at least \*\*6.0 in each component\*\*, or an equivalent accepted test. A degree taught in English may also be accepted, subject to conditions.

\## How to apply

1\. \*\*Find a supervisor or an advertised project.\*\* Edinburgh asks applicants to identify one before applying, so browse the School of Informatics research groups in security and privacy.

2\. \*\*Apply for the PhD\*\* on the official programme page. There's no fee.

3\. \*\*Apply for scholarships.\*\* Check the School of Informatics and University scholarship pages, and submit any separate scholarship applications on the portal.

Apply \*\*early\*\*. Scholarship decisions are usually made well before the start date, and you need an application in place to be considered.

\## Documents you'll usually need

\- \*\*Research proposal\*\*

\- \*\*CV\*\*

\- \*\*Academic transcripts\*\* and degree certificates

\- \*\*References\*\*

\- \*\*English test result\*\*, if required

\## Tips for a strong application

\- \*\*Contact a potential supervisor first\*\* with a short email, your CV and a one-page research idea.

\- \*\*Make your research proposal specific\*\* to cyber security, privacy or trust, and connect it to your supervisor's work. Read our guide on [how to write a research proposal](/guides/how-to-write-a-research-proposal/).

\- \*\*Show research experience:\*\* a master's thesis, publications or security projects all help.
