The **Stanford Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship (SURF)** is run by **Stanford Engineering**. Undergraduates spend **8 weeks** doing real research in Stanford engineering labs, with mentoring from faculty and graduate students. It is designed for students who are thinking about a **PhD in engineering**.

## Programme dates

**21 June – 13 August 2027** at Stanford University, California. You must be available for the whole programme.

## What is covered

- **USD 6,000 stipend**, paid when you complete the programme
- **Travel** to and from Stanford
- **Accommodation** for the 8 weeks
- **Meals**
- Summer registration costs
- **Health insurance**, if needed

## Who can apply

- **Full-time undergraduates** at an **accredited university in the United States**
- At least **18 years old**
- **Rising juniors or non-graduating seniors** who have finished at least **two years** of undergraduate study, with at least one semester or quarter left after the programme
- A minimum **GPA of 3.0** (exceptions may be made for strong improvement or special circumstances)
- A real **interest in a PhD** in engineering

**International students** are eligible **only if they study and live full-time in the USA** and can get CPT approval from their university. Students enrolled at universities outside the US are **not eligible**. High school, master's and PhD students are also not eligible.

## Documents you need

- Resume or CV
- Academic transcript
- **Statement of purpose**
- **Enhancing the Learning Community statement**
- **Two recommendation letters**

## How to apply

1. Read the eligibility rules on the official Stanford SURF website.
2. Prepare your CV, transcript and statements.
3. Ask two lecturers or research supervisors for recommendation letters.
4. Apply online through the Stanford SURF website before the deadline.

## Deadline

**1 February 2027**

## Tips

- In your statement of purpose, name the engineering research areas and labs that interest you.
- Show any research, coding or lab experience, even from class projects.
- Ask your recommenders early so their letters arrive on time.
