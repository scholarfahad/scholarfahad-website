The **University of British Columbia (UBC)** in Vancouver, one of Canada's top universities, offers a research-based **Master of Science in Cell and Developmental Biology**. Every student in the programme receives **guaranteed funding**, so you can do a master's in Canada without paying your own way.

> **Two intakes, two deadlines:**
> - **May 2027 intake:** apply by **30 November 2026** (transcripts and references by 15 December 2026)
> - **September 2027 intake:** apply by **31 March 2027** (transcripts and references by 15 April 2027)
>
> **You need a supervisor before you apply.** Start contacting professors now.

## How the funding works

- **Minimum funding of CA$32,500 per year**, for at least **two years**
- Funding comes from a mix of **scholarships, teaching assistantships and research assistantships**
- Your **research supervisor commits** to supporting you financially throughout your training
- Funding generally continues until you finish, as long as your performance is good
- **Tuition is paid from your funding.** First-year tuition is about **CA$10,082 for international students**, and you keep the rest for living costs

For reference, UBC reports that students in this programme received a **median of about CA$35,200** in funding a year.

## Admission requirements

- A **supervisor's firm commitment** before you apply
- **Two reference letters**
- **English test** taken within the last 24 months, if your previous education wasn't in English:
  - **IELTS Academic 6.5** overall, with no band below 6.0
  - **TOEFL 90** (120-point scale), or **4.5** on the new 6-point scale
  - **Duolingo 125**
- **No GRE required**

## How to apply

1. **Explore research labs** in UBC's Cell and Developmental Biology programme and find professors whose work matches your interests.
2. **Email potential supervisors** with a short, personal message, your CV and a brief description of your research interests.
3. Once a supervisor **agrees to take you**, apply online through UBC's Graduate School.
4. Ask your **two referees** to submit their letters, and upload your **transcripts** by the document deadline.

## How to email a potential supervisor

- **Read two or three of their recent papers** and mention something specific.
- **Explain your fit:** your degree, research experience and lab skills.
- **Keep it short:** one or two paragraphs, plus your CV.
- **Follow up politely** after a week or two if you don't hear back.

## Tips

- **Start now.** Finding a supervisor can take weeks, especially before the 30 November deadline.
- **Highlight lab experience:** final-year projects, techniques you've used and any publications or posters.
- **Budget carefully.** Vancouver is expensive, so plan how you'll cover rent and living costs from your funding.
