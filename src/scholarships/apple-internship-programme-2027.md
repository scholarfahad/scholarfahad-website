**Apple** offers internships for university students who want hands-on experience at one of the world's most famous technology companies. Interns work on real projects as full members of their teams, in **technical and non-technical** areas such as software, hardware, machine learning, design, marketing, operations and finance.

> **No single deadline:** Apple posts internship roles **on a rolling basis** on its jobs website, and seats fill team by team. **Apply early.** At the time of writing, more than 90 internship roles were open.

## Types of internships

- **Summer internships**
- **Co-op internships** during the academic year

Apple also hires students for part-time roles such as **Apple Support College Advisor** and jobs in **Apple Stores**, in some countries.

## Who can apply

According to Apple, internships are open to students who are:

- **Enrolled full-time** at a university
- Studying for a **bachelor's, master's or doctoral degree**
- In **technical or non-technical** fields

**Important:** internships are based at **Apple offices**, for example in the **United States, Germany, Switzerland, China and South Korea**. Each role is in a specific country, so check that you can **work there legally** or get the right visa. If you're studying abroad, look for roles in the country where you study.

## Is it paid?

Yes. Apple internships are **paid**. Pay depends on the role and country. Some listings describe the pay as "competitively salaried."

## How to apply

1. Go to **jobs.apple.com** and filter by **Students → Internships**.
2. Choose your **country** and the **team** that fits your studies.
3. Read each role's **requirements** carefully.
4. Apply with a **CV that highlights your projects**, skills and results.
5. If a team picks your CV, you'll usually have a **recruiter call**, then **several team interviews**, often including technical questions for engineering roles.

## Tips for a strong application

- **Show what you've built.** Apple values projects: apps, code on GitHub, research, designs or business results.
- **Apply to several roles** that match your skills, not just one.
- **Tailor your CV** to each role's description, using its keywords.
- **Prepare for interviews** by practising coding problems (for tech roles) and explaining your projects clearly.
- **Beware of scams.** Apple never charges applicants, and real Apple roles are only on **jobs.apple.com**.
