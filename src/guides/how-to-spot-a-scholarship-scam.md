Scammers know that students want funding badly, and they use that to take money and personal information. Here's how to recognise them.

## Seven warning signs

1. **They ask you to pay to apply or to "secure" an award.** Official government scholarships like Chevening, DAAD, MEXT and Türkiye Bursları are free to apply for.
2. **They guarantee you'll win.** No genuine agent, page or consultant can guarantee a scholarship, admission or visa.
3. **You "won" something you never applied for.** Real scholarships don't select people at random.
4. **The email comes from a free address.** Messages from Gmail or Yahoo accounts claiming to be a government or university are a red flag.
5. **The website address looks slightly wrong.** Compare it carefully with the official site linked from our listings.
6. **They pressure you to act today.** Scammers create urgency so you don't stop to check.
7. **They ask for your bank PIN, passwords or card details.** No scholarship provider needs these.

## How to check an offer

- Find the scholarship's official website yourself, not through the link in the message.
- Contact the university or embassy directly using details from their official website.
- Search the scholarship name together with the word "scam" to see if others have reported it.

## If someone claims to be Scholar Fahad

We will never ask you for money to apply for a scholarship. If anyone messages you claiming to be from Scholar Fahad and asks for payment, report the account and let us know through our [contact page](/contact/).
