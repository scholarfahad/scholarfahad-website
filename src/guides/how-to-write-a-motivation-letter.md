A motivation letter (also called a statement of purpose or personal statement) explains why you want a programme, why you're ready for it and what you'll do afterwards. Committees read hundreds, so a clear structure and specific examples make yours easier to remember.

Most motivation letters should be one to two pages. If the scholarship gives a word limit, stay within it.

## A five-paragraph structure

1. **Your opening.** State what you're applying for and the main goal that drives you. Skip quotes and life stories that start in childhood.
2. **Your background.** Choose one or two experiences from your studies or work that prepared you for this programme. Show results, not just duties.
3. **Why this programme.** Name specific modules, professors, labs or features. Explain how each one fills a gap in your skills.
4. **Your plan after graduating.** Describe the job, project or change you'll work on, and where. Scholarships funded by governments care a lot about this.
5. **Your close.** Summarise in two or three sentences why you are a strong fit, and thank the reader.

## Weak versus strong sentences

<div class="table-scroll">

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| I am passionate about public health. | Over two years at a district health office, I tracked vaccination coverage for 12 communities and saw how poor data delayed outreach. |
| Your university is world-famous. | The module on health information systems and Dr X's work on mobile data collection match the problem I want to solve. |
| I want to help my country. | I plan to return to the Ministry of Health to design a digital reporting system for rural clinics. |

</div>

## Common mistakes

- Sending the same letter to every programme
- Repeating your CV instead of explaining it
- Writing about the scholarship's money instead of your goals
- Spelling the university or programme name wrong
- Going over the word limit

## Before you submit

Read it out loud. Cut any sentence that could appear in anyone else's letter. Then ask someone who knows you to check that it sounds like you.
